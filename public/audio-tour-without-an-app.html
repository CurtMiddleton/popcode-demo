A self-guided audio tour with no app and no QR codes

Visitors tap a card at your entrance, point their own phone at any work and hear about it in their own headphones. No app to download, no handsets to hand out, and no code or number on the wall beside the art.

How it works

Visitors use their own phone and headphones. You don’t hand anything out.

Tap the card by the door

One small card at the entrance opens the tour in the phone’s browser, by NFC tap, a QR code or a short web address.

Point the phone at a work

Any work, in any order. Popcode recognizes the artwork itself, so there’s nothing posted beside it.

Listen

A 30 to 60 second stop plays in their headphones, with a transcript underneath. The phone can drop to their side while they look.

Then choose what’s next

Your buttons appear when the stop ends: inquire about this work, become a member, give or plan a visit.

Questions

Do visitors need to download an app?

No. The tour opens in the phone’s own web browser. There’s nothing to download, no account to create and no password to remember.

Is there a QR code or number beside each work?

No. Popcode recognizes the artwork itself, so visitors just point their phone at a work. The only code in the room is on one small card at the entrance.

Which phones work?

Any recent iPhone or Android phone with a camera, in its own browser. iPhone XS and later and most Android phones can open the tour by tapping the NFC card at the entrance; anyone else can use the card’s QR code or type the short address.

What if a visitor has no headphones?

Every stop can carry its transcript, shown under the player, so visitors can read along. That also serves visitors who are deaf or hard of hearing.

Does it work for sculpture?

Popcode recognizes flat work: paintings, photographs, prints, drawings and textiles. A list for choosing a stop by title, for sculpture and other objects, is coming.

Who records the audio?

Whoever knows the work best: you, your curator or the artist. Record on a phone or upload an audio file. If nobody wants to be the voice, we can make a natural-sounding AI voice from your script.

What does it cost?

Pilot pricing this season is $1,500 for a single show (regularly $2,500), $3,900 for a gallery year (regularly $6,500) and $7,500 for a museum year (regularly $12,000).

How fast can we go live?

Fast enough for a program that changes every month. Once we have photos of the works and the audio, we build the tour, test it on real phones and have it ready before the opening.
