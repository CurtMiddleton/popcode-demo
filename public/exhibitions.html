The Fountain, Villa Torlonia. John Singer Sargent painted it in 1907, in the garden of a villa outside Rome.

The two figures are his friends, the painters Jane and Wilfrid de Glehn. Jane sits on the stone balustrade at her easel; Wilfrid leans back and watches.

Look at her white smock. Almost none of it is pure white: it's cream, pale blue and green, picking up the sky and the garden around her.

Why visitors like it

It feels like having the artist or curator beside you, without the headset at the front desk.

  • Eyes on the art, not the phone.

    It's audio. Once a stop starts, the phone can drop to their side while they look.

  • No crowding at the wall label.

    Everyone hears the story from wherever they're standing, so nobody queues to read six lines of small print.

  • Their own headphones.

    Earbuds they already carry. Nothing to rent, and nothing for you to buy, charge, clean, or lose.

  • Any order, no route.

    Start with whatever catches their eye, skip what doesn't, and go back to a favorite.

What your space gets

  • Buttons after every stop.

    Up to three, chosen by you: inquire about this work, become a member, give, plan a visit. They appear when the stop ends, while the work is still in front of them.

  • A dashboard.

    Which works people stopped at, how far they listened, and which buttons they tapped. Finally, numbers for the show, not just the opening.

  • Your branding.

    The tour opens on your name, your show title and your image. Visitors feel they're with you, not on a tech platform.

  • Live this week.

    Once you have the audio, setting up a show takes an afternoon. That suits a monthly program, an art fair booth or open studios.

  • Transcripts for accessibility.

    Every stop can carry its text, shown under the player, for visitors who are deaf or hard of hearing, or who forgot their headphones.

  • Prints that play at home. Optional

    Gift-shop prints and postcards of the works that play the same stop when visitors get home.

The entrance card

One card by the door. Nothing beside the work.

A small placard at the entrance carries your show's title and three ways into the tour. It's the only code in the room.

  1. Tap.

    An NFC tag inside the card opens the tour. iPhone XS and later and most Android phones read it with no app.

  2. Or the small QR code.

    For phones without NFC, or visitors who'd rather use the camera.

  3. Or the short address.

    Printed under the code, like popcode.app/yourshow, or your own web address.

Locked tags. Every NFC tag is write-protected after it's set up, so nobody can point it somewhere else.
