How it works

One photo, one video, one ornament. It takes a few minutes.

Pick a photo

Upload the photo you want on the front of the ornament and crop it to the circle.

Add the video or voice

Choose the clip the photo should play, or record a message in your own voice.

We print and ship it

The ornament is printed with its short address, like popcode.app/our-first-christmas, on the back, and shipped to you.

Point a phone at it

Whoever gets it opens that address on their phone and points the camera at the photo. The moment plays. Nothing to install.

Questions

Does the person who gets the ornament need an app?

No. They open the short popcode.app address printed on the back of the ornament in their phone's web browser, allow the camera, and point it at the photo. It works on iPhone and Android, with nothing to download and no account to make.

What can the ornament play?

Any video clip or voice recording you choose: a toddler's first steps, a toast from a wedding, a grandparent reading a story, or a message recorded just for the person opening it.

Can I change the video after the ornament is printed?

Yes. The ornament stays the same, but you can swap what it plays from your Popcode account at any time, so next Christmas it can play something new.

How long does the video stay online?

Every Popcode comes with five years of hosting from the day you make it. Before that runs out, Popcode emails you so you can renew it or download your photos and videos.

What does it cost?

You pay for the ornament. The video linked to anything you buy from the Popcode Shop is free. The current price is on the ornament’s page, and shipping is added at checkout.

When should I order for Christmas?

Each ornament is printed to order and then shipped, so order early in December to leave time for printing and delivery, especially if it is going to another state.

Do you ship in the US?

Yes. Popcode is a US company and ships to addresses across the United States. The ornament is printed in the US.

Which photos work best?

Photos with plenty of detail and contrast are the easiest for a phone to recognise. Very plain, very dark or mostly-sky pictures are harder.
